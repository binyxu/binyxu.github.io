About me

Hi, I’m Binyan! I am a Ph.D. candidate in Information Engineering at The Chinese University of Hong Kong, advised by Prof. Kehuan Zhang. Before that, I received my B.Sc. in Automation from the Qian Xuesen Honors College at Xi’an Jiaotong University and studied EECS as an exchange student at UC Berkeley.

My research asks how capable models become reliable, long-running agents. I make an agent’s operating state explicit, turn interaction traces into reusable experience and skills, and externally verify model behavior when internal signals cannot be trusted. I have led four accepted and four under-review CCF-A papers as first or co-first author.

Research interests

My research centers on long-horizon autonomous intelligence and its safety boundaries, including:

  • Self-managed context and agentic RL: exposing token budget, recency, and working-state signals so agents can manage long trajectories and tool interactions.
  • Experience and skill learning: separating temporary retrieval from durable memory, and distilling multi-agent workflows into efficient single-agent skills.
  • Model-agnostic verification: using independent VLM/CLIP-based auditors to verify behavior without relying on a target model’s internal state.
  • Autonomy risk and foundation-model security: studying transferable attacks, training-data integrity, generative backdoors, and the deployment economics of autonomous systems.
